# Clinical content notes

Reviewed 9 September 2026. These notes document the editorial decisions behind this web adaptation.

- The current manuscript for submission is definitive, ahead of earlier tools, working drafts and teaching folders.
- ACE-T is nurse-focused and covers initial bedside actions after a positive delirium screen or recognition of suspected delirium. It complements medical assessment and wider multidisciplinary care.
- The three domains are Acute triggers, Patient experience, and Treatment and communication. The wordmark is ACE-T throughout.
- The four-hour target is to complete, initiate or escalate applicable actions after the positive screen. Urgent concerns require immediate action. Actions can occur in parallel, and four hours is not a validated safety threshold or an instruction to delay.
- The bedside page retains Table 1's substantive prompts, with some neighbouring items grouped for readability (hydration/nutrition, family information/leaflet). It explicitly retains the 4AT score, delirium or suspected delirium, relevant findings, communication of the delirium plan, and discussion of actions with the team.
- The user's requested `Document 4AT score and delirium` wording is used, while acknowledging other locally used screening tools. No independent test scoring or patient-data collection is implemented.
- The infographic is the supplied, paper-aligned v2. The fuller HTML bedside guide is the accessible text version and includes the more detailed Table 1 prompts.
- The two previously removed infographic statements are not reintroduced. The 4AT page instead explains positively that a score of 4 or more suggests delirium and should prompt clinical assessment and a response.
- Generic care context is checked against NICE CG103 and the official 4AT guide. NICE's critical-care/recovery-room exceptions are retained. No prescribing regimen or routine psychotropic treatment is suggested.
- Do not claim effectiveness, mortality benefit, improved bedside care, or four-hour adherence from this developmental pilot. The study examined perceived usability and documentation, with important design limitations.
- Only a short source citation is public. No unpublished manuscript file, correspondence, source attachment path, patient-level information or private working material is distributed.
- The site's external links are informational resources, not endorsements or affiliations. No institutional logos or approval claims are added.
